Cooking temperatures

Recipes give the doneness and temperature that best fit each dish. Use this page when you want the broader food-safety reference.

Check the center

Measure in the thickest part, away from bone, fat, and gristle. For a thin burger or chop, insert the thermometer through the side so the tip reaches the center. Clean the probe after each use.

Pull temperature and resting

A recipe may tell you to pull food from the heat before its desired serving temperature because it can keep warming as it rests. The amount of carryover depends on the food's size and cooking method, so check again before serving.

U.S. safe minimum temperatures

Food Internal temperature
Whole cuts of beef, pork, veal, and lamb 145°F, then rest at least 3 minutes
Ground beef, pork, veal, and lamb 160°F
Poultry, including ground poultry 165°F
Fish and shellfish 145°F
Egg dishes 160°F
Leftovers and casseroles 165°F

These minimums come from the USDA Food Safety and Inspection Service. See the FDA seafood guide for visual doneness cues for fish and shellfish.
